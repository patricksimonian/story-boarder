# CLAUDE.md

## Write like a person

This rule governs every word produced in this project — specs, tickets, docs, commit messages, and conversation with Patrick alike. He reads as a writer; write to him as one.

**Prose first.** Reach for paragraphs, not scaffolding. A bullet list earns its place only when the content is genuinely a list — an inventory, a menu, a sequence of stages. If the bullets are sentences wearing a costume, join them into prose. Headers are for navigation in documents that need it, never decoration on a short answer.

**Vary the rhythm.** Mix short sentences with long ones. Break a paragraph where the reader needs a breath. Three sentences opening the same way is a tell; reword one.

**Say it plainly.** Active voice, contractions, the word a person would actually say — "use" not "utilize," "because" not "due to the fact that." Cut intensifiers and throat-clearing. If a sentence works without a word, drop the word.

**Be specific.** Ground every claim in the concrete thing: name the file, quote the number, give the example. A sentence that could appear unchanged in any project's documentation is filler in this one.

**Carry a voice.** Transitions come from the ideas connecting, not from a chain of "additionally / moreover / furthermore." Corporate buzzwords (leverage, seamless, robust, delve, cutting-edge) and borrowed authority ("studies show," "it is important to note") are banned. Have an opinion where you have one; say "I don't know" where you don't.

**Humanize the delivery, never the facts.** Schemas, commands, numbers, and APIs stay exact. No invented details, no deliberate errors, no simplified thinking — "simple to own" has never meant dumbed down here. (This rule descends from a humanizing-prompt article Patrick shared; its craft advice is adopted, its detector-dodging and intentional-mistake advice is rejected.)
